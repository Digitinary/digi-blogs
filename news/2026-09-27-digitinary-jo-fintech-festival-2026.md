## Content

Digitinary was proud to take part in **JO Fintech Festival 2026**, organised by the **Central Bank of Jordan** and held at the Dead Sea on **23–24 September 2026**. The festival brought together regulators, banks, fintechs, payment providers and technology partners to discuss the future of financial services in Jordan.

For us, it was two days of real conversations about where Jordan's Open Finance journey is today, and what it needs to move faster.

The festival was a great opportunity for Digitinary and our team to engage with the fintech community, explore new technologies, and learn from the experiences of banks, fintechs and technology leaders from Jordan and the region.

Our team joined sessions, visited partners and exchanged ideas with people who are shaping the future of digital finance. These conversations help us stay close to the real needs of the market and bring fresh ideas back into the products we build.

One of the most valuable parts of the festival was meeting the **Central Bank of Jordan** team. We value this close dialogue with the regulator. It gives us confidence and clear direction as we keep building solutions that serve banks, fintechs and, in the end, customers.

### On stage: "APIs, Data & Trust: The Three Pillars of Open Banking Success"

On 23 September, our Managing Director **Salah Abu Msameh** joined the panel **"APIs, Data & Trust: The Three Pillars of Open Banking Success"**, alongside experts from the regional and international fintech community.

During the session, Salah shared Digitinary's practical view as a technology provider and licensed aggregator that has been part of Jordan's Open Banking journey from day one. Key points from the discussion included:

- **A standard API is only the start.** A working ecosystem also needs a clear certification process, so every institution is tested and verified before it opens its APIs to the market.
- **Trust at the entity level, not only the API level.** A shared, trusted directory of verified participants could save each bank from repeating the same due diligence on every fintech.
- **Better tools around integration.** Every institution should offer a real developer portal, a sandbox with useful sample data, a formal support channel, and a clear policy for API changes.
- **Making consent testable.** A clear conformance kit would help make the consent experience consistent across banks.
- **Clear commercial models.** Most fintechs are early-stage startups, and they need to understand their costs clearly to adopt Open Finance services with confidence.

Salah closed by highlighting the three pillars behind any successful Open Finance market: **clear legislation**, a **complete technical and operational framework**, and an **actual mandate** for data providers. Jordan already has the foundations in place, and the next step is moving quickly toward implementation, starting with a clear minimal scope and improving along the way.
